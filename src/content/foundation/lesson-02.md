# Lesson 2: What is Product Management?

## Learning Outcomes

After this lesson, you will:
- Define **Product Management** and the role of a Product Manager.
- Distinguish Product Management from Project and Program Management.
- Understand how PMs align product vision with user needs and business goals.
- Recognize key responsibilities (roadmaps, strategy, feature decisions).

---

## Why This Matters

Now that you know the program flow, you need to know *what* you’re learning. Product Managers (PMs) are the stewards of products. This lesson lays the foundation for all future work – knowing the PM role ensures you focus on the right problems and outcomes.

---

## Theory

At its core, **product management** is *“the process of guiding a product through its lifecycle”*, ensuring it meets customer needs and business objectives. A Product Manager is often described as the CEO of the product: they define the vision, gather requirements, and coordinate teams (engineering, design, marketing) to build the product. Unlike a Project Manager (who oversees execution) or a Program Manager (who manages multiple projects), the PM *owns the product’s success* end-to-end.

Key functions of a PM include:
- **Vision & Strategy:** Set the product’s long-term direction.
- **Roadmapping:** Plan features and releases.
- **User Research:** Understand customer problems (as emphasized by *The Mom Test*).
- **Collaboration:** Act as a bridge between teams to deliver the product.

According to industry experts, understanding users and aligning with goals is essential. For example, product teams conduct **continuous discovery** and frequent experiments to validate ideas before building.

---

## Example

Consider a popular app (e.g. a food delivery service). The PM must decide what new features to build (say, adding grocery delivery) based on user feedback, market trends, and business strategy. They would research, plan a roadmap, then work with engineers to execute it.

---

## Case Study

Spotify’s product manager famously switched from no-offline to offline mode after realizing users often lacked consistent internet. This decision came from listening to user needs and aligning with the vision of reliable listening. It's an example of putting user goals first.

---

## Summary

Product Managers ensure the *right product* is built. They balance **user needs (desirability)** with **business viability**. This lesson introduced the key responsibilities and mindset of a PM. Keep these ideas in mind as we begin building your own product thinking.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Product management = owning a product’s strategy, roadmap, and outcomes.
- PMs **bridge teams** and focus on user needs (not just features).
- Effective PMs validate ideas before building (continuous user research).
- The PM role differs from project/program roles in scope and ownership.
