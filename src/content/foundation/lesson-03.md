# Lesson 3: How to Learn Product Management

## Learning Outcomes

After this lesson, you will:
- Understand the **learning cycle**: Lesson → Quiz → Assignment → PM Lab.
- Appreciate *learning by doing* (hands-on projects) as the fastest way to gain PM skills.
- Know how to use feedback (quizzes, mentors, peers) to improve.
- Plan a schedule and set a weekly learning goal.

---

## Why This Matters

Product Management itself is learned by **practice**. Just as you wouldn’t become a chef by only reading recipes, you can’t master PM without building products and solving real problems. This lesson shows you how to use this program effectively and establishes habits for success.

---

## Theory

The PM OS curriculum is designed around a **cycle of experimentation**. Each module has:
- **Lessons (theory):** Short readings or videos on a topic.
- **Quizzes:** Quick checks to ensure you understood key points.
- **Assignments:** Practical tasks that result in a portfolio artifact (your deliverables).
- **PM Lab:** Open-ended exercises that develop problem-finding skills.

This mirrors how real PMs work: generate ideas, test assumptions, and iterate. We encourage you to set weekly goals (e.g., “complete all tasks by Sunday night”) and reflect on your progress. According to educational psychology, active learning through projects leads to deeper understanding. In product terms, we run small experiments and learn from data – the same applies to your learning process.

---

## Example

Imagine you learn product design by joining a hackathon. You quickly prototype a feature, then test it with users. You’d learn fastest by doing. Here, your assignments (like interviewing a user) are mini-hackathons in product thinking.

---

## Case Study

After using this approach, a student *Rahul* not only grasped PM concepts faster but also filled his portfolio with practical work. His proactive goal-setting and regular reflection (in PM Labs and reflections) kept him motivated.

---

## Summary

We covered *how* to use this fellowship: by *doing*. Remember to approach each week like an experiment: try tasks early, learn from mistakes, and ask for feedback. This way, you build real skills and artifacts for your resume.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Learn PM by **building**: complete projects, not just reading.
- Use quizzes and feedback as opportunities to improve, not just tests.
- Manage your time: set clear goals and reflect on outcomes each week.
- Embrace a curious, data-driven mindset: test assumptions and learn from results.
